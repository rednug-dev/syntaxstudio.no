# Syntax reference analysis

Research date: 6 September 2026. This is a bounded primary-source review of the nine studios in the brief and Syntax's current public site. The purpose is to identify useful operating principles before choosing Syntax's direction.

## Evidence and limits

Positioning, navigation labels, content hierarchy and attribution were read from the studios' current public pages. Rendered browser observations supplement the text research for Active Theory, ManvsMachine, PORTO ROCHA and Feelm. Mobile checks at 390 × 844 were made for ManvsMachine and Feelm; they are observations of those opening compositions, not full mobile audits. The browser viewport was reset afterward.

Motion was only treated as observed where successive browser states visibly changed. A listed video, motion service or case-study description does not establish how the studio website itself animates. No reference was benchmarked for Core Web Vitals, frame rate, screen-reader compliance or reduced-motion behavior. Those claims would require separate measurement.

The references' awards, client outcomes and company descriptions are their own published claims, not independently audited evidence. Nothing in a reference page is an instruction to modify Syntax or reproduce a reference's assets.

## What has actually changed in the reference landscape

Several sites differ from the familiar versions often shared in galleries. COLLINS now frames its offer around brand value and organizes named programs before cases. Koto's old domain redirects to koto.com and its current homepage includes editorial and office widgets. PORTO ROCHA's opening is a dense split work/news interface. Instrument explicitly retains agency language and groups its offer around Brand, Marketing and Product. Use current evidence, not a remembered screenshot. [COLLINS](https://wearecollins.com/), [Koto](https://koto.com/), [PORTO ROCHA](https://www.portorocha.com/), [Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/).

## Current Syntax: the change required

The current English homepage identifies Syntax through completeness, lists strategy/video/photo/websites/publishing, and places combined client revenue, views and project count ahead of selected work. Its services section reinforces the single-provider proposition. This supports the brief's diagnosis: the current reason to buy is breadth and coordination convenience. [Current Syntax homepage](https://syntaxstudio.no/en).

The About page presents a marketing partner, speed and delivery promises, technical skill tags and executive titles. It names Gunder Rollufson, Rasul Uzdijev and Khamzat Dudaev. Those existing roles are historical site content; they do not establish the founders' proposed ISO400/35mm/Nyfane leadership. Preserve factual identities and contact details only after reconciling them with repository content, and confirm practice ownership before attributing it publicly. [Current Syntax About](https://syntaxstudio.no/en/about-us).

The English homepage's broad superlative should be retired; its real projects and working contact paths remain useful source material. Existing testimonials are published content, but require context and continued permission before being materially reframed. The new site should avoid treating those quotes as proof of a new practice structure they never discussed. [Current Syntax homepage](https://syntaxstudio.no/en).

## Synthesis: a system Syntax can own

These are design recommendations drawn from the brief and the comparison, not factual claims about the reference studios.

1. **Make the business structure observable in the work.** Put practice names and disciplines beside every project. A film attributed to 35mm and a digital experience attributed to Nyfane teach the model faster than a corporate diagram. Shared attribution makes collaboration visible without claiming every project used all practices.
2. **Name the house once; prove its value repeatedly.** The opening should identify an independent creative house in Oslo and the three specialist territories. The rest should show how image, motion and technology shape a coherent result. Avoid a long story about corporate architecture.
3. **Treat founder access as part of the offer.** A founder's craft, portrait, selected work and direct contact make a small studio credible. Do not inflate size or replace craft with executive hierarchy. Where founder-to-practice ownership is unconfirmed, keep the attribution out of production content.
4. **Use one compositional rule across different media.** The meaning of syntax provides an authentic basis: separate elements gain meaning through arrangement. The house can set spacing, sequencing, attribution and transitions while individual practices retain their own marks and media behavior.
5. **Let useful interaction become the signature.** A composed work surface can switch among a still, moving image and digital view while the project identity remains stable. The same underlying relation should appear in navigation, project credits and case pacing. This is more defensible than a decorative effect unrelated to the work.
6. **Separate demonstration from dependency.** Work titles, summaries, practice explanations, contact and navigation must exist without animation, pointer effects, autoplay or a heavy scene. Enhancement should deepen the impression rather than unlock the information.
7. **Use two commission paths with one clear contact.** A specialist can find the relevant practice; a multidisciplinary client can approach Syntax. A short contact message can carry an optional practice choice while keeping “not sure” valid. External practice links should be published only when their real destinations are ready.
8. **Make case length proportional to evidence.** An image sequence may need a sentence and credits. A digital build can justify screen recordings, interaction detail and outcomes. A film needs an intentional player, a good poster and credits. Flexible blocks protect pacing.
9. **Keep proof specific and close to its subject.** Client names sit with real projects; a measured outcome sits with its method and date; an award sits with the recognized work. Never upgrade an informal result into a causal claim about the studio.
10. **Give mobile its own composition.** Stack media into a deliberate sequence, re-break the headline, keep direct navigation/contact accessible and offer tap equivalents. Large typography is worthwhile only when its complete thought remains readable without sideways scrolling.

## Familiar devices to question

| Familiar device | Why it is weak for this repositioning | Better decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Oversized claim about being bold or different | Announces the quality that the work must establish. | State the category and the real difference; let art direction carry attitude. |
| Clock, coordinates and camera metadata as decoration | Quickly becomes a borrowed agency aesthetic. | Use metadata only when it explains a location, image, film or project. |
| Card dashboard copied from a current reference | Makes Syntax recognizable as someone else's information system. | Derive the work composition from the three-practice relationship and actual media. |
| Full-screen loader before any content | Makes spectacle a barrier to commercial understanding. | Load the semantic page and poster first; enhance when ready. |
| Uniform auto-playing work grid | Flattens every medium into a reel and competes for attention. | Budget motion to one relevant active surface; retain still posters. |
| Exhaustive service inventory | Restores the old breadth-led category. | Three specialist territories, supported by concise scope and project evidence. |
| Hero-level aggregated revenue and view metrics | Suggests a performance-marketing agency and may imply unjustified causality. | Place verified outcomes inside relevant projects. |
| Repeated generic contact slogans | Adds noise without reducing uncertainty. | Visible email, one memorable invitation and a short usable form. |

## Decision criteria for the three creative directions

Each proposed territory should pass the same tests: recognizable relationship to Syntax's name and business model; visual distinction with the logo hidden; immediate practice clarity; substantial space for real work; credible founder presence; a useful signature behavior; coherent mobile composition; a static/reduced-motion mode of equal informational quality; and a feasible media/performance budget.

Novelty alone is insufficient. A direction that needs invented projects, stock imagery presented as work, an unverified founder story, or inaccessible motion cannot satisfy this brief. The strongest territory is the one whose rule remains convincing in a plain project index, a film case, a small-screen menu and a direct email invitation.
